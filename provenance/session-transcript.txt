  example is min="6h"). That's the mode for "run this for X hours". Two things
  to know about how it behaves:

  - min is a floor, not a hard cap. The Stop hook keeps auto-continuing until at
    least X wall-clock has elapsed, then lets the in-flight turn finish. Expect

    together. Workaround: put the done-criteria in the prompt body and tell the
    agent to keep hardening / expanding scope until the clock runs out, while
    tracking progress in .codex/loop/<name>/state.json so restarts are safe.

  First, what to avoid

  The top picks I'd give a crack at right now

  1. Gyarmati-Hennecart-Ruzsa sum-difference constant (C3a) - best straight 
  shot.
  Find sets U of integers maximizing log|U-U| / log|U+U| via base-b digit
  constructions; a lemma converts any good finite U into a certified lower
  bound. The record went 1.1584 (AlphaEvolve) → 1.1740 (base-21) → 1.1835
  (base-33) → 1.1873 unverified (base-89, capped) in months, and the pattern is
  screaming: the (base, digit-set) search space is barely explored and the
  certificate is exact integer counting, pure Python, seconds to verify. Upper
  bound is 4/3, so there's a mile of headroom. Deliverable: a new certified
  lower bound + PR to teorth/optimizationproblems. I'd put real odds on a record
  within days of iteration.

  2. Chvátal-Sankoff constant, binary alphabet (C31a) - the "unverified record" 
  arbitrage.
  Verified lower bound: 0.792665992 (2024). Current record 0.79970 is by an
  autonomous agent and flagged unverified on Tao's site. Two shots, both local:
  (a) produce a verified Lueker-style coupled-DP certificate beating 0.7927 (the
  method is exact dynamic programming; the constraint is RAM and state-pruning
  cleverness, which is exactly an iterate-locally problem), or (b) rigorously
  certify/refute the 0.7997 claim using their published code. Either outcome is
  a real contribution to a 50-year-old constant.

  3. Sidon density in (4,5)-sets (C5b = Erdős #757) - genuinely overlooked.
  Bounds 9/17 vs 4/7 were just moved in Feb 2026 by two humans; no evolve-farm
  has touched it. The upper bound is a finite construction: exhibit a (4,5)-set
  whose largest Sidon subset is small. That's a SAT/ILP search with trivial
  exact verification. Medium odds, but it's an Erdős-numbered problem where a
  laptop find would directly tighten a published 2026 bound.

  4. Kernel optimization in the new pair-correlation framework - the profound 
  one.

  What this problem is

  The Chvátal-Sankoff constant γ₂ is a 50-year-old open problem (posed 1975):
  what fraction of two random binary strings can you match up as a common
  subsequence? It has a Wikipedia page, appears in several textbooks, sits at
  the crossroads of probability, combinatorics, and the LCS algorithms used in
  bioinformatics and diff tools, and its exact value is unknown. Simulations say
  ≈0.8122, but nobody can prove it.

  Three difficulty tiers, and where we landed

  1. Finding the exact value: genuinely hard, famous-problem hard. Steele
     conjectured 2/(1+√2) in 1986; that stood 23 years until Lueker's bounds
     disproved it. Solving γ₂ exactly would be a real event in probability
     theory. We did NOT do this.
  2. New methods for bounds: medium-hard. Dančík's automaton idea (1994) and
     Lueker's coupled value iteration (2009) were the conceptual steps, each a
     solid publication (Lueker's is in JACM, a top venue).
  3. Pushing the bounds with existing methods + serious compute: medium, 
     engineering-hard. This is our tier, and it's the same tier as Heineman et
     al. 2024, which was a legitimate publishable paper for moving the lower
     bound at exactly this level.

  So how cool is it, really?

  - It's a real record on a real named constant. If it holds up to community
    scrutiny, the interval on the Wikipedia page literally changes: [0.792666,
    0.826280] becomes [0.792666, 0.824443]. First movement of the upper bound in
    17 years.
  - The reason it was gettable is target selection, not genius. Everyone since
    2009 (including the 2024 paper and the AI agents farming this constant)
    piled onto the lower bound. The upper bound is the same machinery run on the
    dual side, and nobody had pointed modern hardware at it. Our contribution:
    noticing the neglected side, plus the matrix-free/symmetry/fixed-point
    engineering to run it two scale steps past 2009.
  - The verification standard is genuinely strong: exact integer feasibility
    over 400M states, negative control, stdlib-only replayer, independently
    re-run. That's above the bar of the starred AI claims on Tao's scoreboard,
    and it's why this one can be submitted with a straight face.
  - On the hype scale: this is not Claude's 41.6%→67.2% zeta jump (new mechanism
    on a central object), and not the unit-distance disproof (imported theory,
    famous conjecture). It's a solid workmanlike record: the kind of thing that
    earns a scoreboard entry, a short arXiv note, maybe a Wikipedia edit, and a
    "nice!" from the five people who care deeply about LCS constants.

  Caveats before we brag: it needs outside eyes (the certificate is
  machine-checked but no human expert has reviewed it), and "record" assumes
